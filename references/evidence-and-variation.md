# Evidence and Variation

Use this reference when deciding whether an observed habit is a core rule,
current preference, legacy strength, or conditional choice.

## Corpus basis

The style model was derived from 14 papers with received/writing evidence from
2016--2024:

- 8 IEEE TAC papers;
- 5 Automatica papers;
- 1 IEEE CDC paper;
- early, transition, and recent recency masses of 21.1%, 19.2%, and 59.7%.

Recent work has greater default influence through a 5-year half-life, but early
papers remain evidence for persistent principles and strong limitation writing.
Normalize quantitative features within each paper and applicable module before
aggregation. Recency is not a quality score or confidence level. These are
coauthored papers, not evidence of who personally wrote each sentence.

## Persistent core

- exact property-change novelty;
- obstacle -> mechanism -> guarantee;
- multi-part contribution alignment across modules;
- dependency-first result and proof organization;
- assumptions as proof contracts;
- Remarks as an interpretation layer;
- plain technical verbs;
- examples tied to theorem-level claims.

## Current preferences

- functional segmentation in recent Abstract examples, without a uniform
  sentence-length or stage-count trend across every paper;
- tighter mapping from enumerated limitations to design architecture;
- more visible structural isomorphism across contribution, theorem, proof, and
  evidence;
- self-contained repair when an earlier proof mechanism has a specific gap.

These preferences do not override mathematical clarity.

## Legacy Best

Early papers often state technically precise limitations and future directions.
Retain that practice when supported.  Do not omit a useful limitation merely to
imitate a short recent Conclusion.

## Conditional choices

- `we` versus method/result as grammatical subject;
- present tense versus prospective `will` for staged exposition;
- paper-level or local section roadmap;
- formal `Problem 1` label;
- dedicated preliminary-lemma section;
- limitation/future-work paragraph;
- number and placement of Remarks;
- long versus split sentence;
- sentence-initial `-ing` by grammatical function;
- placement of assumptions before the objective, after it, or at first use;
- construction-first versus enabling-lemma-first result organization;
- in-place proof, checked citation, or an existing Appendix proof;
- prose transitions versus numbered proof steps;
- Remarks before or after the main theorem, with coherent secondary functions.

## Rejected rules

Do not enforce:

- no `we` in the Abstract;
- no prospective `will`;
- a fixed sentence length;
- a fixed number of contribution items or Remarks;
- an absolute ban on roadmaps;
- a mandatory future-work paragraph;
- a blanket adjective/adverb ban;
- a blanket sentence-initial `-ing` ban;
- the historical claim that the author never names a concept;
- a universal construction -> theorem -> proof -> Remark order;
- moving all effective gain or domain conditions out of theorem statements;
- expanding every proof or omitting every `similar` proof;
- a requirement that a Remark contain only one analytical category;
- a fixed Conclusion sequence, sentence count, or tense throughout;
- treating a possible extension as ongoing or completed work;
- requiring an already drafted Introduction before writing a Conclusion;
- a fixed Introduction paragraph order or a mandatory separate contribution list;
- requiring a proved failure mechanism for every research gap;
- treating an unaddressed setting, an uncovered case, and method impossibility
  as equivalent;
- a mandatory Abstract limitation opening, fixed mechanism/guarantee order,
  or stock simulation ending;
- equating a new property of an existing concept with a newly proposed concept;
- venue-specific citation, heading, or proof-ending formats as personal style.

## Confidence discipline

Keep three categories separate, as detailed in
[language-style.md](language-style.md): corpus observations, editorial
recommendations, and the user's explicit no-coinage constraint. The user's
impressions about modifiers and `-ing` were hypotheses subsequently submitted
for empirical checking, not permanent bans. Standard grammar checks are not
evidence of an individual author's style.

The language reanalysis covers 14 complete Abstracts (83 sentences), plus
retrieved body-text candidates. POS rates are automatic estimates. Confirmed
body examples establish counterexamples, not exact full-text frequencies.
There is no other-author comparison corpus; do not claim stylistic uniqueness
or personal aversion from these data. The six-paper formulation calibration is
a bounded structural comparison, not full-corpus annotation or independent
evidence from six unrelated research programs.
The subsequent result/proof calibration inspects 15 PDF pages in six papers
and labels 14 selected formal results. It records presentation choices, not
proof correctness, full-paper frequencies, or independent-author preferences.
The Conclusion calibration checks all 14 complete Conclusions against 16 PDF
pages, including the continuations of C02 and C05. All 14 use retrospective
present perfect; 12 include future or ongoing work, of which three explicitly
report work in progress. Five explicitly identify a current constraint in a
separate statement; this strict count excludes an assumption mentioned only
as a future relaxation (C06), scope stated without a limitation judgment
(C11), and limitations of prior work. These are paper-level descriptive
counts, not causal evidence of stylistic growth or a basis for mandatory slots.
The Introduction calibration checks the complete narrative Introductions of
all 14 papers against 26 PDF pages, excluding separate Notation/Nomenclature
from the rhetorical sample. Section-by-section roadmaps appear in 13/14
(C10 omits one); visually separate numbered/bulleted contribution lists in
0/14. Inline enumeration has several distinct purposes. C03, C06, C12, and
C13 include displayed system models within the narrative. These observations
support conditional format choices, not a formula ban or a list prohibition.

The Abstract calibration rechecks all 14 complete Abstracts on their 14 PDF
first pages, using the same 83-sentence, 2,009-lexical-token sample as the
language reanalysis, not a new independent dataset. Eight open with prior work;
six start with the current problem or contribution. Five end with examples
or simulations; other endings state guarantees, comparisons, an application,
or additional theoretical results and their possible uses. All are single
paragraphs with 4--9 sentences; C14 contains an explicit author-year citation.
Neither an example ending nor a citation-free format is a universal rule.
The same coauthorship, related-research, and venue confounds still apply.

When a source draft lacks evidence for a strong comparison, weaken or defer the
claim.  When a style choice has competing corpus examples, select by the
current module's function rather than by raw frequency.
